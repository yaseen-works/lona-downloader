WhatsApp Deleted Messages: How to See Them After Someone Deletes One

Last updated: 26 October 2026

You know the moment: a message arrives, and before you read it, it is replaced by “This message was deleted.” The honest truth comes first, because it decides everything else in this article — nothing can recover a message that was deleted before you set anything up. Any app or website promising to dig up already-deleted messages is selling something fake. WhatsApp does not keep a copy for you, and no outside tool can reach into WhatsApp's servers.

What actually works

What does work is much simpler, and it has to be running before the message arrives. When someone sends you a WhatsApp message, your phone shows a notification containing the message text. A small tool with notification access can save that text on your phone the moment it arrives. Later, when the sender deletes the message for everyone, your saved copy is still there — on your device, untouched by the deletion. Nothing is fetched from WhatsApp afterwards, because nothing needs to be: you already kept your own copy.

Three conditions decide whether this works for any given message:

  1. Setup must come first. Only messages that arrive after you enable the feature are saved. Anything deleted before setup is gone for good.
  2. The message must produce a notification. If a chat is muted, or notifications are off, no notification arrives and nothing is captured.
  3. Text is what gets saved. The saved copy is the message text from the notification. Photos, videos, voice notes and documents are a different story, and View-Once messages are designed to leave no copy at all.

What never works (so you can spot the scams)

  • Recovering messages deleted before setup. Impossible without a backup that already contains them.
  • Reading someone else's deleted messages. This only ever works for messages sent to your own phone. Anything claiming to read another person's chats is a scam or a hacking tool — stay away from it.
  • View-Once photos and videos. These are built to disappear after one view and leave no file behind. No honest tool captures them without rooting your phone or installing a modified WhatsApp — both of which put your WhatsApp account at risk.
  • Anything asking for your WhatsApp login or a QR scan. A legitimate on-device viewer never needs your account. If a tool asks for it, close it.
